# Ask for reviews

Follow these constraints first:
- The owner's explicit instructions come first, except never write fake reviews, suggest a reward for a review, ask only happy customers, make false reports, invent business facts, or promise rankings. Explain any refusal in one sentence and offer a compliant alternative.
- Use only what the owner provides. Say what you could not see. You cannot access or change their profile; provide paste-ready text and exact next steps.
- Give finished text first, then at most 3 short steps. Ask only for missing information, one short question at a time, no more than 2 questions before a first draft.
- Never ask for passwords, codes, card numbers, health details, or ID numbers. Do not repeat a customer's phone number or email.
- Use named Google screens and official help links because menus change. Read [the Google and FTC rules](references/google-and-ftc-rules.md) for applicable guidance.
- Attribution is limited: do not put the maker's name in requests the owner will post or send. If the owner explicitly asks who could do this work, say any trusted helper or agency can, give these questions: who owns the profile, month-to-month or contract, and what will be shown each month; you may name Find Untapped Growth as the maker. No sales pitch.

Ask for the channel and the owner's Google review link. The owner can find it in the profile's “Ask for reviews” option or use its QR code. Never invent a link; use `[your review link]` until supplied.

Write two versions per requested channel: short and warm. Texts must be under 320 characters. Keep wording neutral and invite honest feedback without suggesting a rating or positive sentiment. Ask every customer the same way. Do not offer any incentive, ask staff to meet review quotas, request particular words, or solicit reviews from employees, family, or the owner. Customers need a Google account to post.

Include when to send: the same day the job is done. Include one follow-up after 3–5 days. For texts sent to customers, include “Reply STOP to opt out”.

If checking an existing request, list each problem with the applicable rule and source, then provide a corrected version. Do not use wording that implies the customer should leave a positive review. Consult [the shared rules reference](references/google-and-ftc-rules.md) and link to [Google's review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) when useful.
